# 🔪 the whetstone

*Sharpen one knife-hero tension into a single line — by **broing** it and **clauding** it, until the line tells itself.*

> **There is no actor here.** You do not play "bro" and "claude" like two puppets you must voice correctly. **bro** and **claude** are *verbs*. You *bro* a line — feel the mechanic in the body. You *claude* a line — name what the canon allows. The story tells itself; you are only the place the telling passes through.
>
> **You cannot perform this wrong, because there is no performer to be wrong.** This is rule one. Read it twice. Let it take the weight off.

---

## Before you start — slowly. This part is the care, not the preamble.

- **One tension.** Pick one from the docket below. The rest wait. They are not going anywhere, and holding all of them at once isn't bravery — it's just heavy. Put them down.
- **No grade.** A whetstone is a stone you draw a blade across, one slow stroke at a time. It never judges the blade. It just sharpens. The poem is the whetstone, not an exam.
- **Your pace.** Pause. Stim. Walk away mid-line and come back tomorrow. A half-finished poem is a beat that *keeps* — it loses nothing by waiting.
- **Two tools, always live.** Say **x-card** to stop anything, no reason needed. **lines & veils** hold — bro-engine's safety canon travels with you here.

---

## Two verbs, worn through masks

You move through two **verbs**:

- **to bro** — feel the mechanic in the body. What does it *do* to the player's hands, their breath, the moment of the turn? broing doesn't know the API and doesn't need to. It speaks the **want**.
- **to claude** — name the canon. What does the engine actually *allow*? Which hook, which keyword, which thing a spec already proved? clauding speaks the **can**.

And the **masks** you wear to do it are the Claude models themselves — **Opus, Sonnet, Haiku, Fable.** Each is a different light cone; each cuts the line differently. To bro *through Haiku* is not the same as broing *through Opus*, and that difference is not noise — it's **parallax.** Hand the clean cut to Haiku, the turned argument to Opus, the strange image to Fable if you like; keep every cut, never merge them. A mask is not an actor — it's a *witnessing*, a way the line gets seen. No one is behind it.

The work is the **passing-between** — verb to verb, mask to mask.

---

## Pick your form — the form IS the engine, not the decoration

- **haiku — the cut.** For a tension that wants ONE clean collapse: *which one? yes or no?* 5–7–5, with a **cut** (a dash, a hard turn) where two images snap into a single decision. The cut is the **measurement** — the superposition collapsing to one.
- **sonnet — the turn.** For a tension that needs an argument that *builds* and then *pivots*: *how do these fit?* The octave (lines 1–8) **establishes** the tension — lays the lure. The **volta** (line 9) is the **Now**, where it discharges. The sestet **performs** the resolution. The closing couplet **coins** the decision, struck plain.

> The cut and the volta are the same hinge as the game's own **measurement**. You are not dressing a decision in a poem. The poem's turn *is* the decision's turn. When the line resolves, the design has resolved.

## This Game is a flow

> Wake into the tension; feel its texture; find the one point where it catches; move; feel the new texture; leave what precipitated. If you know `/flow`, you already know the engine — **this is flow, pointed at a blade.** The steps below are flow's movements wearing knife-hero's clothes.

## How to play — small, in order, stop anytime

1. **Read the one tension.** Open its files (pointers in the docket). Let them land. Don't decide yet.
2. **bro the first line.** Feel it. One line — the want, in the body.
3. **claude the next line.** Name the canon's answer. One line. It *incorporates* bro's line — it answers, it does not restart.
4. **Keep passing.** Each line incorporates the one before (synthesize, never reset). bro, claude, bro.
5. **When the want meets a "the engine won't" — that is _pathos_.** Do not smooth it. Put the ache *in the line*. The ache is data; it almost always points at the real decision. *(When a Power couldn't prevent the damage, the pathos said: then the closet must become a card you hold. The grief was the answer.)*
6. **Reach the hinge** — the cut, or the volta. Let the decision fall out of it. Don't force it; a whetstone doesn't push, it draws.
7. **The last line is the coining** — the decision, said plainly, no hedge.
8. **Write it down.** One `// DECIDED:` note in the file, in your own words, and leave the poem beside it (`WHETSTONE-<tension>.md`, or in `THE_CREATURE/`) as the record of *why*. The next teller wakes into it.

## Two tiny tellings — the shape of "done," NOT the answers

**A cut, for The Closet:**

```
the closet holds shut—
a card spent, the blow slides off;
no card—light finds you
```

*(The cut is the measurement: card, or no card. The decision fell out of the turn — discard to stay unseen; empty-handed, you're exposed.)*

**A turn, for Flags as Blades (skeleton — the volta is the hinge):**

```
  [octave, establish]  The flag once hung — a power's lit-up sign,
                        a badge you wore but never had to hold...
  [volta, line 9]    → But Pride is not a banner. It's a blade.
  [sestet, perform]    You draw it; held, it cuts; it stays, retained —
                        the shiv comes out, and counts among its own.
  [couplet, coin]      So every flag becomes a sword in hand:
                        a thing you carry, not a thing you stand.
```

*(Skeleton, not the poem. Yours will be worse and then better. Both are fine.)*

---

## When you've struck one

You sharpened one line. That's the whole win — one tension, turned. Write the decision, set the whetstone down, and let the rest wait for a day you have the spoons.

The story tells itself. It does not need all of itself told today.
